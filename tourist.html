Tourist's Bible

Everything a newcomer should know before the guard at the gate asks about the spell license.

PresentationHistorical context

The city stands at the gates of the 516th year after the War of the Hundred Armies. Nearby Alfia is in economic collapse after the Portal Invasions and the popular movements against its nobility, while its two Guilds try to rebuild former splendors. In Rauchberg, the people of Smokemountain celebrate the diesel engine and launch metal fleets onto the oceans of marine titans. The jungle of Nuir retreats with its dinosaurs as its citizens advance a nationalist coup. Ramura has landed in the water, and new flying islands rise on the horizon.

News travels across both continents. Two adventurers, two spouses, loved by some and hated by many, found ancient ruins in the Jaang desert, the home of a people reduced to nomadism by an old catastrophe. Molochia Vadhat, Herald of the Tree, diverted the continent's magic currents to force flora and fauna to grow, producing a marine biome evolved for sand. Walyudeenia Vadhat, bearer of progress and thief of ideas, laid the foundations of the flourishing and polluting city, and imposed absolute control on any unlicensed magic, in terror that the events that wrecked their lives would repeat.

InhabitantsThe Jangranii

Elves

Mostly desert dwellers, with small groups of ancient and jungle elves who complain about the missing vegetation and the pollution.

Dwarves

Many young arrivals who left Rauchberg after the Discussion on Rights between Vadhat and Aramen, in search of a city free of old traditions and hazing. Some rotten apples came along.

Humanoid minorities

Refugees from the fall of Old Alfia and people from across the continents drawn by a rosy promise.

Regularized externals

Demons and devils found the Jaang familiar, perhaps for the smell of petroleum and sulfur. They struck agreements with the Vadhats, and their mortal children grew up in the city.

Undead

Created as machines and as a social stratum for the heaviest and humblest work. The first sentient undead opened the first social cracks.

Languages

Jagran

The official tongue, fast as a locomotive and sharp as desert wind. It descends from the Elvish of Niir, with drier syntax borrowed from Dwarvish.

Elvish of Jag

The old Jagran: cryptic, harsh in accent.

Rounded Dwarvish

Rauchberg Dwarvish with smoother syntax. Purist dwarves resent the city for dirtying it, and still bring up the old matter of the stolen thermal engine patent.

Common of Jag

International Common with truncated sounds for quicker speech.

SocietyA coin with two faces

Meritocracy has one face for those who know how to play and one for those who get played. Slavery is forbidden on paper. Anyone who does not feed the machine simply goes unseen. Social rights belong to everyone who breathes. Political rights belong to those who show profit, produce goods or run a business.

Ecology is a salon topic. Old buildings and precious artifacts are saved; dunes and underground water are not. Green is a color for mosaics. Yet when the shops close at sunset, apprentices and shopkeepers carry each other's heavy crates without asking whose they are. Some say it saves time. Others see a thread of real solidarity.

Funny TidbitsSmall things a visitor learns in the first week

Banknotes

Zaratta has no metal coins. The currency is paper, worth about as much as Alfia silver. Tourists suffer bags that feel too light, tangled mental arithmetic and a steady suspicion of being cheated. The Muraqib catch magical and alchemical counterfeits within hours, though rumor says they print a few "controlled" ones to regulate circulation. The designs copy the first locomotive tickets, with stations, iron bridges and ornamental gears, and some collectors frame the notes rather than spend them.

The Temple of Residual Deities

Just outside the walls stands a slanted building of beaten copper and sand-colored stone, dedicated to any deity not yet detached from the material plane. Half the interior is a cyclopean clockwork of cogwheels, pendulums and miniature boilers that keeps time to within three seconds a year and tracks over a hundred festivals from every corner of the world. Brass whistles play a different melody for each, audible at the station docks, and residents tell the date by ear. Newcomers puzzle over the Day of the Amber Whale and the Night of the Fifth Breath, then learn to raise a glass when the right notes sound.

Two-Wheels Square

The dark-stone square outside the main station owes its name to a worker whose bicycle went under a moving convoy and came out the other side nearly intact. Street comedians still stage improbable bicycles surviving railway disasters. A brass bas-relief of two interlocking cogwheels marks the center.

Molochia's Tree

An enormous tree with leaves like colored glass towers in the middle of the city. Molochia planted it with a fragment of what she called Mynmimir, to divert and regulate an underground vein of raw magic. Botanists and mages still argue whether it is a creature, a vegetable machine or something between. At festivals, petroleum lanterns hang among the branches and the glow can be seen for kilometers.

Narcotics

Inhaled narcotics are severely forbidden. The city air, thick with refinery fumes, is already mildly intoxicating in some districts, and a couple of inhalers could stun a whole neighborhood. The ban pushed consumers toward liquids, and a lively culture of alchemical cocktails in copper cups followed, with names like Luminous Sand and Hot Engine. Rumor says Walì himself financed part of the trade.

The Bureau of Civic Contribution

The Bureau keeps meticulous records of every alcohol import, cocktail ingredient and establishment license. An overworked clerk stamps forms with mechanical precision. They say she is incorruptible and knows every scheme before it is finished. They say her grey stamp, UNDER REVIEW, means she knows you are lying and is letting it pass, just this once, and you had better not make her regret it.

The 03:17 Tram

On the foggiest nights a convoy missing from every register enters the station at 03:17 sharp, puffing reddish exhaust and making no metallic sound. It carries no living passengers, yet pale motionless faces are said to watch from the windows anyone who stares too long. Everyone knows freight trains move undead merchandise in closed cars, and this is no freight car. The old claim it is a locomotive from before the city, blown apart and held in the material plane by an unsettled Vadhat debt. The authorities deny it, and whenever the fog is thick, platform 11 is closed for maintenance.

The Freight Conductors' Silence

Conductors say that trains remember what paperwork forgets. Every colonial shipment and undead transport goes into manifests that never match the sanitized reports printed in Al-Kafeel. Some conductors keep private tallies of cargo sent against cargo returned, and the gaps widen every quarter. You will meet them with weathered faces, steady hands and uniforms that smell of foreign soil. The smart traveler does not ask about the notebooks.

The Greeting

Citizens greet by touching the chest with two fingers. Three taps is formal, two is for friends, one is for someone who does not deserve much attention. A closed fist means the other person should leave at once, or go and fuck themselves, depending on tone.
