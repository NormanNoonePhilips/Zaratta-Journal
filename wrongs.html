Artistic Patronage

In Zaratta even beauty is a transaction. A bronze statue costs 500 in materials and 200 in labor, yet the patron records 2,000. The artist receives 700. The other 1,300 exists only on paper.

  • The Gallery Circuit: the artist kicks back part of the price under the table.
  • The Phantom Commission: a sculpture is paid on paper and delayed forever, until the artist has "moved abroad".
  • The Collaborative Inflation: five patrons each claim 2,000 for a 5,000 monument.

Real artists resent a system that calls a masterpiece and a laundering scribble equally priceless. Bureau assessors choose between replacement and complicity.

The Anti-Monopoly Cold War"Better three competitors than one corpse."

Where spending equals voting power, a monopoly over bread or water would be a dictatorship. No statute forbids it, yet monopolies hardly ever form. The moment one firm grows too dominant, rivals receive mysterious funding, warehouses burn, strikes break out and sudden safety inspections arrive. Large ventures are built as consortia so that no partner can weaponize a share without wrecking its own investment. Some monopolies are tolerated as infrastructure: the Central Bank, the railway network, the art guilds and, disputably, water distribution.

The ambitious aim to be first among equals in an oligopoly. Once a consortium bought six rivals and took seventy percent of meteor excavation. Within two months a dozen researchers vanished. Walì reportedly left his office at midnight in his old alchemist's gear, the official papers reported a spontaneous combustion, and by year's end the consortium was bankrupted by a price war.

The Fuel Economy

For 150 years Zaratta has been the main fuel supplier of the surrounding nations. Rauchberg's bio-paste reactors last longer and Alfia experiments with arcane batteries. The refineries still outproduce everyone, for now. What happens to a city built on oil when the oil runs out is a question nobody wants to ask, least of all the Vadhats.

The Vadhat Voting MysteryWhy do they not simply vote?

The family could outvote the whole council. They have not voted in seventeen years. Ask one directly and the answer arrives in one of three forms. The gentle deflection steers you to the new aqueduct until your question evaporates. The aristocratic dismissal quotes the Family Rules and says goodbye. The dreadful silence is a stare that suggests you asked why the sun rises. The official reason is "for the good of the City". They do not need to vote, since the city bends to their gravity anyway.
