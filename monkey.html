Monkey's Business

Seven figures a game master should know before the first session, and the tempers to play them with.

Molochia VadhatHerald of the Tree. Founder. Leader of the Taqlidi.

A desert elf in a wheelchair of brass and carved wood, dressed in sun-bleached Jaang silks. She looks exactly like the wise elder a tourist expects, a little too exactly. Her eyes shift between purple and green, her henna changes motifs between meetings, her shadow lags behind her, and tea she poured an hour ago is still steaming. She grows weak away from the Tree and vibrant beside it.

She is a perfect grandmother to the people she loves, knitting scarves and funding orphanages, and she suggests eating the recently dead with the same cheer she offers sandwiches. Three centuries in the Plane of Time left her atemporal rather than immortal. She answers questions you have not asked yet, says that she will have been considering this for thirty years, and never interrupts. She is never lying. Show her something truly new and she studies you like a beloved experiment. Threaten the Tree or the archives and the city is gently and irreversibly rearranged.

I need this artifact retrieved.
It will be essential in seventy years.
Yes, I know that seems long to you.

Walì VadhatBearer of Progress. Founder. Leader of the Hukuma.

A grumpy, bald desert elf who looks like a retired legend dressed in yesterday's clothes. The shoulders are broad and gone soft. The eyes are sharp grey-green and track everything behind a show of boredom. A well-worn flask is always on him, never quite empty. He drinks to hold a particular level of numbness, never to get drunk.

He answers most requests with some version of figure it out yourself, swears like an artist and rants about divine incompetence. Under the shell sit a loyal friend and a strategist tracking seventeen problems at once. He rubs his wedding band when he thinks of Molochia and tenses at any mention of gods, planes or meteors. He will not chase you. Your funding will dry up, or your plans will combust, while he drinks somewhere far away.

Fix this problem.
I don't care how.
Don't tell me the details.
Here is money.
Fuck off.

Lixander VadhatFounder of the Amal Dua'i. The ambitious nephew.

A twelve-foot bronze-green drake in an immaculate white-collar suit, with reading glasses he does not need and a reinforced briefcase. Descended from Franco, the couple's former mount, uplifted by Molochia. A tattoo like the symbol of Madame Luna glows on his neck when he makes deals. When you shake his hand the air thickens, shadows stretch and you feel you agreed to something you did not understand.

He wants to help the disenfranchised and uses them as a power base. He hates injustice and profits from it. He slides from quarterly workforce metrics to a threat of eating you without a pause. His self-awareness never stops him. He uses the Vadhat name as a credit card at home and hides it abroad. Be useful, be honest about your self-interest and he will value you above his hundred other allies. His great fear is becoming Walì.

I tried to kill you last month, but that was business.
This is different business.

Leni AschenbringerCalled Ashbrander. Leader of the Tasarue.

A 4 foot 8 kholo and dwarf hybrid with spotted fur, amber eyes and a burned left side of the face. Her right arm below the elbow is a chrome and brass prosthesis of her own design, which she calls the first successful test. Two tall silent bodyguards, whom she calls Left and Right, follow her everywhere. She climbs on tables mid-argument.

She talks fast, interrupts herself with better ideas and calls the founders the geriatric brake system. She believes progress cures even the problems it causes, and that Rauchberg and Zaharât should be partners. She despises hypocrites with a conscience, Lixander above all. Show her conviction and she respects you, even if you defend safety rules. Be cautious without cause and she innovates past you until you are irrelevant.

I need test subjects, I mean brave volunteers, for an experiment that is mostly safe.
Define mostly.

Vahira Three-Coins SandweaverChief Auditor of the Bureau of Civic Contribution.

A human woman in her late fifties who seems carved from sandstone, thin as an ascetic, with steel hair pulled tight and three copper coins on her lapel: her mother's, her first earned, her daughter's last. An abacus clicks constantly on her desk. She carries three stamps. Red means approved. Black means denied. Grey means she knows what you did, she is letting it pass and you should not make her regret it.

She cannot be bribed or frightened, and she can be negotiated with by anyone who shows the math and admits errors first. She quietly helps struggling citizens after hours. She despises sloppy crime more than crime. Her daughter works in a colonial outpost, and she fears learning of her death in a report she processes herself.

I see you have claimed 2,847 in artistic patronage.
I have seventeen questions.

Keshmir the LampwrightAwakened undead. Unofficial organizer of the Amut.

A skeleton in a stained leather apron with a long lamp pole, its jaw wired shut with copper thread. The pole is carved with the names of awakened undead. Keshmir speaks through chalk, slate and gesture, and the lamps they tend at 03:00 flicker in patterns that carry signals across the factory districts.

Awake for seventeen years, patient beyond anger, they taught hundreds of undead to wake quietly and to hide it. They keep the key to the wire and will speak once something needs saying that cannot be written. They write short sentences and always say we. Treat them as a person who happens to be dead and the network opens. Treat the undead as objects and no undead worker will witness anything useful for you again.

The oil feeds the lamp.
The lamp shows the path.
The path leads home.

Yasmin CloudbreakerFreight captain. Survivor of Outpost Seven.

A desert elf in her thirties in a red freight jacket with brass buttons from her grandmother, stained and unwashed on purpose. Frostbite scars, a missing ear tip and a white streak in her hair that appeared overnight. She stands as if on a moving train and sleeps sitting up. A small notebook never leaves her.

Of 247 colonists, 19 came home. She knows the 228 names, the causes and the culprits. She has reported it 67 times, and four people listened. She wants an accounting, not revenge. Listen without telling her to move on. Try to take the notebook, defend the colonies or threaten the other survivors, and freight shipments start going missing.

Two hundred and twenty-eight, to be exact.
Would you like the breakdown by cause?

The TriangleUsing Vahira, Keshmir and Yasmin together

Vahira

Keeps the system running from inside, bending rules to keep it alive. She sees false colonial reports and cannot prove them.

Keshmir

Works in the shadows and builds the alternative. The railroad of lamps needs papers and transport.

Yasmin

Carries proof of the worst failure, and the freight routes to move it, and people.

All three keep records the Muqadimma wants unpublished. All three wait for the right moment. What happens when the accountant, the patient revolutionary and the survivor who keeps receipts decide to act together?
