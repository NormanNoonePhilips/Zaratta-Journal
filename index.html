The ArrivalFirst sight of the city, from a carriage window

Desert and cutting wind. Golden dunes to the horizon, and among them the long shadows of drakes and bulettes. Then the landscape begins to change.

Not at once. It changes the way poison changes a courtier's favorite wine. Cacti grow arms like coral fans. Stone-skinned fish leave soft trails in the sand. Anemones as large as carts open and close their mouths over careless desert birds. The whole scene looks like a sea with the water taken out, the miracle and abomination Molochia Vadhat left behind when she bent the continent's magic veins.

The train whistles twice. First come the smokestacks, tall and thin, spitting green smoke and then purple, as if the air were trying new recipes. The smell of burning bitumen hangs over everything. The city lives on the thick black oil it digs from beneath the desert, and that oil drives presses and locomotives alike.

Then the towers of glass and metal, looking down on older stone houses. Bare beams and exposed bolts beside hypnotic ornament. Workshops covered in jewels, palaces that resemble machines, machines that resemble palaces. At the station the crowd takes the visitor in: dwarves with lenses welded to their eyes, desert elves in light veils, kholoi with lacquered claws, demons in crooked ties, and a choir of kobolds rehearsing on brass wind instruments.

At the gate the guard asks three things. Pass, checked. Spell license, present. Religious declarations, none. Inside, the walls speak: thousands of black and white cyanotype photographs, added daily by citizens and tourists, a common diary without order or hierarchy.

Money must flow like the desert wind, or it becomes sand.

The Oath of FoundationThe original text, as sworn

Zaharât stands as a Meritocratic Republic founded upon the survival of the City and the dignity of work. Sovereignty resides with the City itself, which exercises this authority within the forms and limits established by this Oath.

We are a family, forged in bureaucracy.

The City recognizes and guarantees the inviolable social rights of every citizen, both as individuals and within the social formations where their personalities develop. In exchange, the City requires the fulfillment of mandatory duties: labor, economic solidarity, and social solidarity. All citizens save the Vadhats possess equal social dignity and stand equal before the law, without distinction of sex, lineage, language, political opinion, personal condition, or social condition. Religious opinion falls outside these protections. The City bears the duty to dismantle economic and social obstacles that limit the freedom and equality of citizens, ensuring the full development of each citizen and the effective participation of all workers in the economic and social organization of the Republic. Political organization remains excluded from these guarantees.

To live, to earn the right to work, to earn the right to buy, to earn the right to say, how to earn the right

The City exercises its sovereign authority through the Muqadimma, a deliberative council constituted by representatives whose eligibility is established through documented Contribution to the Republic. Contribution is contingent upon demonstrated participation in the City's spiritual and productive life, as verified by the Muqadimma itself.

We built the lock and the key.

The Vadhat family, as founders of Zaharât and pillars of the City, exists above ordinary law. Members of the Vadhat family operate under the Family Rules, statutes designed to prevent internal insurrection, magical catastrophes, and other threats to municipal stability. One may enter the Vadhat family through birth or adoption, but none may renounce it. Spouses remain outside the family unless adopted by explicit decree of the sitting Patriarch or Matriarch. For the good of the City, complaints against the Vadhat family from ordinary citizens, or from any Vadhat save the sitting President, will not be tolerated.

The City's worth is made from our sacrifices.

The City recognizes the right of all citizens to work and actively promotes conditions that make this right effective. Every citizen bears both the duty and moral obligation to perform, according to their abilities and choice, an activity or function contributing to the material, structural, or spiritual progress of society. Religious opinion does not constitute spiritual progress.

We wrote a new people policy, no more defined to a god or king's property.

Zaharât officially acknowledges the material existence of deities and divine entities as documented fact, not matters of faith. However, the City classifies such beings as extradimensional aliens of hazardous nature, possessing unquantifiable political and military capabilities that threaten municipal sovereignty. All religious confessions stand equally dangerous before the law: organized worship therefore constitutes collaboration with foreign powers of unacceptable magnitude. Religious confessions operate as de facto embassies to entities beyond the City's jurisdiction and control. For this reason, the same reason Zaharât would not permit a foreign army to garrison within its walls, religious organizations cannot be permitted to organize, proselytize, or exercise political influence in any form.

To be faithful is to pray in what you invest.

Nevertheless, citizens retain the right to private prayer and personal belief as inviolable right. The City lives on by both body and spirit, and so Zaharât retains the duty to nurture the arts, information and culture of its people as archstone of the community. Zaharât abhors censorship and the act of Damnatio Memoriae as tool of treachery, believing in the right of everyone to know their surroundings.

On the City's account accounting interested on the citizen's interests.

Zaharât formally rejects the Convention of Vëthurgard and its "Collection of Universal Rights of Humanoids" as discriminatory, exclusionary, and fundamentally incompatible with the City's founding principles. The Convention's limitation of rights to those categorized under "Animity" would strip the majority of Zaharât's non-humanoid citizenry of legal protection and recognition. This rejection is non-negotiable and reflects the City's unwavering commitment to a meritocratic society where contribution and capability, not arbitrary biological taxonomy, determine one's standing.

May We rest, employed.

SummaryQuick facts for the hurried reader

Identity

Founded about 150 years ago by two adventurers, Walì and Molochia Vadhat. A meritocratic, proto-capitalist republic with social rights for every living citizen and political rights only for those who contribute.

Technology

Dieselpunk. Petroleum, compression engines, diesel locomotives, cyanotype machines, banknotes instead of coins, and strictly licensed magic.

Government

The Muqadimma, split into seven competing factions. See People's Rights.

Tone

Noir politics, Arabian Nights and industrial revolution, with cosmic horror waiting at the edges.

Themes

  • Progress against preservation: science, history and magic.
  • Exclusion dressed as meritocracy.
  • Beauty and utility fighting for the same street.
  • The unseen: undead labor, a rewritten desert, forgotten colonies.
  • The Vadhat family, blessing and curse of the city.
