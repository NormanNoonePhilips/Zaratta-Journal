People's Rights

Who sits on the council, what each faction wants, and how a citizen earns a voice.

The MuqadimmaA market of tongues and interests

"The Muqadimma is like the desert. You can't stop it; you can only hope to cross it without being buried, or immerse yourself and ride it."

Nobody in Zaratta admits to belonging to a party, a word that sounds like an army or a church. There are groups, though: opinions, tribes of ideas. When a proposal reaches the chamber, every eligible representative votes according to weight. They vote on the what and the why, and rarely on the how. Temporary implementation committees of technicians then build the thing. They cannot change its scope, its goal or its budget. Nobody has ever explained how the technicians are chosen, which is itself something to vote on.

HukumaZaratta first. Led by Walì Vadhat.

The city before history, progress and even justice. The Hukuma controls the Central Bank, maintains infrastructure and holds a veto over proposals judged existentially risky. It acts as final arbiter when a dispute threatens to turn violent or when magical experiments approach dangerous thresholds. Its institutional memory covers the whole history of the city, so its governance is cautious, pragmatic and sometimes close to paralysis. When it approves something by silence, as with the colonial ventures, the choice looks calculated: certain failures are allowed to teach.

TaqlidiHistory first. Led by Molochia Vadhat.

Every artifact, structure and scrap of cultural memory must be kept. The faction keeps archives that span several city blocks, reachable only through procedures that can take years. Nothing is shared in either direction. Its agents carry out "artifact retrieval" at foreign ruins and collections without consent, and the resulting incidents hurt Zaratta's standing abroad. Provenance records exist for every item, and those records are restricted as well.

TasarueProgress first. Led by Leni "Ashbrander" Aschenbringer.

Called Uber Zahnrad among expatriate Rauchberg dwarves. They hold that magic and the gods are fading, so science must be pushed on every front, and any damage repaired later with new knowledge. They sponsor engine research and experimental alchemy, run workshops with minimal safety oversight, and count accidents as the price of innovation. Their leader sees the two founders as shackles on the city. The faction grows among the young and among recent immigrants.

Amal Dua'iThe living first. Founded by Lixander "Vadhat".

"For those who cannot speak, we are their voice!" The nearest thing the city has to a proper party. They organize workers' councils, offer legal aid against the bureaucracy and petition to change the census-based vote. They won several workplace safety reforms and wider public schooling. Proposals on voting rights and undead status keep failing. Rumor says Lixander juggles agreements with folklore creatures and almost-gods of the outer planes. Do not ask what they do with foreign presshouses and churches.

MuraqibOrder first. No single spokesperson.

Police, magistracy and law-drafters, staffed by regularized devils, demons, axiomites and ganzis. Devils handle contracts and long investigations, demons answer chaos and violence, axiomites design legal frameworks, ganzis take magical anomalies. They are hard to corrupt. Their drafts hold layers of interpretation that show up only during enforcement. They promised to make the City's life a paradise and yours a hell.

AlmustaemaratEconomy first. The colonies.

A few decades ago entrepreneurs persuaded the city, with Walì's silent assent, that colonies would feed it in exchange for "exported progress". The extraction was brutal even by local standards. Of seventeen outposts, fourteen are gone, eradicated by locals with their own views on foreign interference. The three survivors send sanitized reports, inflated production figures and omitted casualty lists. The family could demand an accounting and does not. The council could investigate and will not. Nobody wants to lead a faction whose members keep disappearing into foreign soil.

AmutThe undead. Unofficial.

The name comes from "amil" and "yamut": dead tired, or one who works and does not die. The law sees sentient undead as neither citizens nor slaves, since the Oath rejects slavery. Several thousand are thought to exist. They meet in underground work sites, hiding messages in shift changes and transport schedules. They argue over whether to raise a leader of their own or wait for the Bone Archdragon some of them see in dreams. If they organize, they become a workforce nobody can compete with.

Voting and PatronagePower measured by spending

Banknotes allow enormous volumes of exchange, and the city idealizes patronage. Frozen assets mold and rot. What counts is how much you spill back into the city's veins. Each year a citizen must document a minimum of circulated value, roughly six months of skilled wages, through one or more of three streams.

Direct Investment

Funding construction, businesses, public works. The most respected stream, because it visibly improves the city.

Employment Creation

Running a business that employs others. The more people employed, the louder the voice.

Consumption and Commerce

Spending on goods, services and rent. The path for steady earners with no business sense.

Verified names appear in Al-Kafeel, and citizens read the lists like society columns. Most people never vote. A factory worker spends everything on survival, which circulates value yet never reaches the documentation threshold. Skilled artisans and shopkeepers live in the frustrating gap, and some pool resources, three or four to a slot, rotating who casts the vote. The Muqadimma frowns on this and cannot quite outlaw it.
